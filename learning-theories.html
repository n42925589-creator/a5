Learning theories explained

A plain-language guide to the major theories of learning and how they shape everyday teaching.

Why theory matters

Ideas behind the methods

Every classroom routine rests on an idea about how people learn. Knowing those ideas helps teachers choose methods on purpose, rather than by habit, and adapt when something isn’t working.

No single theory has all the answers. Most effective teaching blends insights from several.

Key concepts

Ideas worth knowing

Zone of proximal development

Vygotsky’s idea that learners progress best on tasks just beyond what they can do alone, with support.

Cognitive load

Working memory is limited, so too much new information at once can overwhelm learners.

Scaffolding

Temporary support, such as prompts or models, that is gradually removed as learners gain confidence.

Schema

Organised knowledge in long-term memory that helps us understand and remember new information.

Reinforcement

Consequences that make a behaviour more likely to happen again, such as specific praise.

Metacognition

Thinking about your own thinking: planning, monitoring and evaluating how you learn.

Bringing it together

A blended approach

  • Explain new ideas clearly and in small steps.
  • Model the thinking you want to see.
  • Let learners practise with support, then alone.
  • Use discussion to deepen and test understanding.
  • Teach learners how to plan and check their own learning.
